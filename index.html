How it works

You sell the cover. We take the dispute.

  1. You offer the cover at your checkout

    One sentence above the pay button, backed by calmlyresolve.com. That is what gives your users the confidence to buy and sell things they otherwise would not.

    You set the cover fee and you collect it. Cover applies to every transaction inside the band, so it is not an extra your user has to opt into. Your own dispute process stays your own.

  2. A dispute is valued on what the law says it is worth

    When a dispute arises we check the documents can settle the question, then value the claim on what the law entitles the claimant to: the Consumer Rights Act 2015, read with the contract terms. Both parties receive the same report at the same time.

  3. The method is published and the record is audited

    The valuation method is published and versioned. Anyone can read the rules and check a valuation against them, which is what lets both sides see the process is even-handed. Read the method in full.

    A defendant who disagrees is not arguing with our judgement on their case. They are arguing with a public rule — and if they are right, we fix it for everyone.

  4. We take the claim on and pay your user

    Cash up front, a share of what we recover, or both, as they prefer. No claim is ever presented to your site for payment, so a dispute never reaches your accounts.

Cover runs on transactions worth £200 to £25,000, for sites trading in England and Wales. Some claims are valued but not taken on: a party in insolvency, dissolution or strike-off, a claim below the minimum value, and a claim outside the three kinds this cover takes. The exclusions are stated at the point of purchase and set out in the written agreement.

The fee as a share of the transaction is the number that decides it, not the annual total. It is what your buyer sees. For comparison, Vinted charges buyers a protection fee on every order and event refund protection is typically a single-figure percentage of the ticket.

You keep 75% of what you collect as standard. The wholesale price we charge is a quarter of the standard cover fee for your transaction size, taken out of money already in your account, so nothing is invoiced to you and nothing is charged per dispute. The standard fee is 1% of the transaction, with a minimum of £1 and no more than £100. Founding sites are on different terms.

Two things decide the right fee

What it is as a share of a typical transaction on your site, and what comparable cover costs elsewhere. Marketplace buyer protection and event refund protection both publish their prices. We put those comparators next to your own numbers when we build your benchmark.

Who pays it is your decision

  • Your buyer. An itemised cover fee at your checkout. This is how marketplace buyer protection is normally charged.
  • Your seller. Inside the fees your sellers already pay you.
  • You. From your own margin, with nothing shown to either side.

The arithmetic above holds on all three. Cover applies to every transaction in the band whichever route you pick, because an optional add-on is a different product with different consent rules. Which route suits your site is your call, and we have not decided it for you.

How your own figure gets made

From the first covered transaction, transactions are assigned at random to a covered arm and an uncovered arm. Your buyers see no difference. At the readout you have your own number, on your own data, under a protocol agreed in writing before it runs.

What the published evidence says, since you will ask. Nobody has measured whether dispute cover makes more people buy, on any marketplace, including ours.

What you earn does not rest on it. You are not buying an uplift. You are selling a product at a fee you set, and keeping the larger part of what you collect. That holds at any volume, whether or not one extra person buys.

The closest study is eBay’s platform-wide switch to buyer protection in 2010 (Hui, Saeedi, Shen and Sundaresan, Management Science, 2016). Buyers’ willingness to pay rose 2.9% — and 3.7% where the seller had no top-rated badge, against 1.3% where they did. Nearly three times larger where the buyer does not already trust who they are dealing with.

The two facts that sit next to it. The closest structural match to a marketplace like yours — a used-equipment marketplace, Roberts 2011 — found no effect at all. And how much this is worth differs by site, because it depends on how much protection your buyers already have. A single number across every marketplace would be wrong, and we are not going to publish one.
