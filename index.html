Who it is for

How it works

For someone who transacts on a covered site.

  1. You transact on a covered site

    The cover is stated at the point of purchase. The site pays for it. You pay nothing, now or later.

  2. Something goes wrong

    You use the site's own dispute process first. Calmly takes no part while that process runs.

  3. The site's process ends in a final refusal

    The site routes the dispute to Calmly and passes its own records, with your authorisation. Those records are the evidence. You gather nothing.

  4. Your claim is checked and valued

    Calmly checks the claim against its admission rules. An independent method values it. The report is yours to read and keep.

  5. The claim is taken on and pursued

    Calmly's pool takes the claim on at its valued price and pursues it. You are paid from what it recovers. Once a committed buyer exists, you can sell the claim at its valued price instead.

The full journey, and what is excluded

What the site pays

And what it does not.

The site pays
  • One fee per covered transaction, monthly in arrears, on its own transaction count.
  • A floor while the readout runs. The published rate applies only once measured value shows the cover has paid for itself.
The site does not pay
  • Nothing per dispute. The fee does not vary with dispute volume.
  • No capital, no reserve, no fund. No claim is ever presented to the site for payment.
  • No exclusivity, in either direction. Either side can stop on a month's notice.

Participants pay nothing at any stage. No figure is published on this site; the rate and its floor are set out in heads of terms, with the arithmetic behind each one.

The standard

Every valuation is produced under a published, versioned standard.

Each report states the version it was produced under. Both parties to a dispute receive the same report at the same time. Changes go through change control, and no unversioned method values a claim. The record of what the method predicted against what happened will be published from the first readout, and checked by an independent accountant under agreed-upon procedures. No institution countersigns an individual report.
